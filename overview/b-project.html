Course Project (CEWA 565)#

The goal of the course project is to give you hands on experience working with data of particular interest to you, as well as experience writing about and presenting data and statistical information.

In your project, you will ask a question (pose a hypothesis), answer it using a variety of analysis techniques, and clearly address the statistical uncertainty of the answer. In the report, explain your objectives, your data source, your methodology, your results, and discuss remaining uncertainties.

Your project will be evaluated through an oral presentation (20% of total grade) and a written report (80% of total grade). The written report will be no longer than 10 pages (for an individual or group of two; or 15 pages for a group of three), including figures (references will not count towards the page limit). I encourage groups of 2 (not one or three) when possible.

Note

Additional Resources UW has services to help people with statistics and data analysis. These are designed primarily to help graduate students engaged in research (not with homework), but presuming your course project is related to a research interest of yours, they have been very helpful in the past.

Potential project ideas#

You are welcome to use your own data and answer a question of interest to you and relevant to your own research. Please let the instructor know as early as possible so that we can make sure you have sufficient data to be successful. Alternatively, you may choose or adapt one of the project ideas below. Multiple groups may answer the same general question, but they should pick different specific data or specific tests to use, which can be coordinated in conjunction with the instructor. Past project examples are available on the class Canvas page under Files > Student Projects and Presentations > Project Examples.

  1. Cloud Cover at Low Tide around Puget Sound Hot summer weather can adversely affect intertidal organisms during low tide, and in the Salish Sea of the Pacific Northwest, summer low tides frequently occur in the middle of the day. Raymond et al. 2022 demonstrated how the timing of low tide at various locations around the Salish Sea during the 2021 Pacific Northwest heatwave led to varying die offs of intertidal organisms. For this project, we want to know whether there have been shifts in the frequency of marine cloud cover particularly during low tides. How has this varied in space and time? To answer this,

  • Acquire timeseries data of air temperature, cloud cover and tides from Washington ASOS stations and NOAA tide gauges Friday Harbor tide gauge and around Puget Sound.

  • Plot example timeseries of these variables for June 2021 and compare with the Raymond et al. paper, link above.

  • Choose three locations, one at the UW Friday Harbor Laboratories, one north of there, and one south of there. For each of these locations, classify low tide as the lowest 25% of the intertidal range. For each warm month (April, May, June, July, August, September), calculate the percent cloud cover and the average air temperature both for the month as a whole and for only these low-tide times. Look for trends or patterns in these monthly statistics over all years of record.

  • BONUS question Perform frequency analysis on the clouds, summer temperatures, and tides. Are there “beating” patterns that cause phase to more or less in synch at different times? We would expect clouds and temperatures to be related, but are they better correlated at daily or hourly timescales? Are tides correlated at all? How?

  • BONUS 2 Washington Department of Natural Resources has eelgrass (Zostera marina) records over time. Interactive data viewer here. Find some ecological observations near your sites and see if they respond to any low-tide weather shifts you observed. If you want more information on eelgrass see this or ask Jessica for more information. There’s also an opportunity to collaborate with researchers at Friday Harbor Laboratories.

  1. Snow and Fires in Eastern Washington Last summer, 2026, was a very bad fire year in Washington State. Total winter precipitation was slightly above normal, due to heavy December rain, but snowmelt was early in spring 2026. Fire risk is typically related to 42-day dry fuel moisture. Was the early snow melt related to the fires or not? Consider the Little Giant Fire, which started on July 16 near Lake Chelan, and the Trinity Snotel station. How does snow melt and disappearance at the Trinity Snotel station relate to the timing of snow disappearance near the ignition Point of the fire? (The latter you can determine from gridded or remote sensing data.) Given June 4th is 42 days prior to July 16, what is the typical snow covered area of the region on June 4th compared to the snow covered area in 2026? How long after snow disappears is soil moisture still high? If you are interested, you could extend this analysis to other fire ignition points relative to Snotel observing stations.

  2. Rain on snow in arctic regions How much does it rain vs. snow in different regions of the arctic, and what data sources can we trust? Evaluate output from CARRA2, ERA5 and MERRA2 against various surface observations. How well can we trust each surface observation? How do you assess accurate data in a remote region?

  3. How does forest harvesting affect flooding? Using data from the HJ Andrews experimental forest, determine whether the risk of flooding has increased following forest harvesting. You may download timeseries data from the class Canvas page or from the HJ Andrews data page.

  • Watershed 1 was a 400- to 500-year old-growth forest, measured from 1953-1961; from 1962-66, it was 100% clearcut, and in 1967, it was burned.

  • Watershed 2 was kept as a control during this time. Using a variety of techniques we learned in class, as well as others you may encounter in the literature (use at least three different tests), determine whether forest removal increases flood risk at different return intervals in the HJ Andrews area. What is your confidence in this (Type I and Type II errors)? How sensitive are your results to the statistical tests you chose? A nice example paper evaluating multiple approaches to peak flow response just came out: Moore et al 2006 . Note that you can use the Penticon Creek data below to look at the flooding question, or you could use the data and scripts linked to this recent paper.

  1. How does forest regeneration (following harvesting) change summer streamflow, or low flows? Similar to question 1, again using the HJ Andrews data. See Perry & Jones (2017) paper on this topic. (Ask the instructor for a copy of this paper if you’re unable to access it online)

NOTE: For both questions 2 and 3, an error was recently found in the HJ Andrews streamflow dataset. Please read the details on their data page. In particular, Watershed 1 had streamflow change by 20% due to a recalculation of the rating curve. The data on our class canvas page is from the old rating curve. As one project, you may research rating curve methods, how and why they were recalculated for Watershed 1, and what impact that recalculation has on the conclusions derived from questions 1 and 2 above.

  1. How does forest harvesting affect streamflow? Using data from the the Penticon Creek Watershed, described here, and available here, assess how forest harvesting affects the streamflow metric of your choice. You may address the same questions as described for the HJ Andrews watershed above, you may compare data between the two regions, or you may ask a question of your choosing. Papers about Upper Penticon Creek are availabe under Files>Optional Reading and References>Upper Penticon Creek, on the class Canvas page. In this dataset, the 240 creek is unlogged and serves as the control. Road construction and clearcut logging began in late 1995, see papers for details. By late winter of 2007, 47% of the 241 and 52% of the Upper Dennis Creek watersheds had been clearcut. Snow and soil moisture data, as well as lidar data for tree heights, are also available.

  2. How does weather vary in a high-elevation valley in Colorado? The U.S. Department of Energy and the National Oceanographic and Atmospheric Administration have collected a lot of measurements in the East River Basin of Colorado, as part of the SAIL and SPLASH campaigns. The data is publicly available for both SAIL and SPLASH. Talk to the instructor if you want to work on a project using these data.

  3. Are high-altitude mountain streams flowing or experiencing ice jams more frequently in winter months in years since 2015 compared to years before? High-altitude mountain streams are typically covered with snow and ice in the winter, with no discharge until snow melts in the spring. However, when rain falls on a frozen stream, ice jams often occur, creating large increases in water pressure that cannot be directly related to discharge. You may download time series of stage, discharge and stream temperature here for different streams in Yosemite National Park, California. Ice jams are flagged and are apparent in the stage data. Using a variety of techniques we learned in class, as well as others you may encounter in the literature (use at least three different tests), determine whether the frequency of winter ice jams is increasing. What is your confidence in this? You may want to consider different months of the year or different regional air temperatures, and you may want to compare and contrast streams at different elevations and of different sizes. (See this guide to learn more about these sites. Also, talk to the instructor if you want more recent data.)

  4. Choose your own adventure…

Grading Rubic#

Presentation#

(30 points; will account for 20% of project grade)

A powerpoint-type presentation, like you would present at a conference, 10 minutes in length, with an additional 5 minutes for questions. Your classmates are your audience. Present your question, why it is important, what methods you employed to answer the question, your major results, and the implications of your results. For pairs, both project partners will present together.

  • Clarity of Overall Presentation (10 points)

    • Clear statement of the problem

    • Clear explanations of what you did, easy to follow

    • Good transitions from one section to the next

    • Ability to answer questions raised by audience

  • Quality of Visual Aids/Graphics (10 points)

    • All graphics easy to see, properly labeled, large enough font

    • Correct limits on plot axes to show relevant information

    • Proper choice of type of graph to convey information

    • Interest level – slides nice/interesting to engage audience

  • Technical Analysis and Discussion (10 points)

    • Proper methods applied

    • Accuracy of results

    • Explanation of why results are what they are

    • Ability to relate results to other studies and other presentations in the class

Written Report#

(30 points; will account for 80% of project grade)

For the written report, you must include an abstract (no more than 300 words) and a list of references; these can be in addition to your 10 (for a group of two; or 15 for a group of three) pages. Follow the same format outlined for the presentation but feel free to include more details (equations, etc.). Please address any questions or comments that were raised during the presentation. Use clear, concise sentences and proper grammar.

  • Quality of Writing (10 points)

    • Clear and easy to read

    • Proper grammar and spelling

    • Clear statement of the problem

    • Good transitions from one section to the next

    • Clarity of explanations of what you did

  • Technical Analysis (10 points)

    • Clearly described methods, proper literature cited

    • Technical difficulty of methods applied

    • Accuracy of results

  • Interpretation, Analysis, and Discussion of Result (10 points)

    • Attempted to explain why certain patterns were observed in the data and/or analysis

    • Thought about results in context of other studies

    • Outlined next steps to be taken

    • Addressed underlying assumptions in the analysis and how these may have affected results
